Magnetic Sector

A magnetic field bends the ion path by mass · the classic, earliest analyzer

A magnetic sector accelerates ions to the same energy, then sends them through a magnetic field perpendicular to their motion. The Lorentz force bends each ion into an arc, and the radius depends on m/z: heavy ions curve gently (wide radius), light ions curve tightly (small radius), so the beam fans apart.

How it works

Adjust the field strength B — only the ion whose radius matches passes through the slit to the detector; the rest hit the wall.

light ion → tighter curve heavy ion → wider curve wrong radius → hits the wall

By scanning the field strength, the sector selects which m/z reaches the detector. It also uses a magnet — but note the difference from FT-ICR: a sector bends ions through a single pass, whereas FT-ICR traps them to orbit many times.
